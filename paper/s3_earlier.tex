\section{Earlier Studies}
\label{sec:earlier}
\textbf{Study 1 (surrogate, protocol v0.1).} On 24 held out synthetic trajectories with a surrogate visual front end and a synthetic heading cue, any heading cue lowered the drift once vision degraded (run level median at the middle level 5.12 percent of path length without a cue, 1.20 percent with a gated cue or covariance matching). Covariance matching was indistinguishable from a chi square gate, a cue bias random walk of 2 degrees per root second made every heading aided system worse than none, and every filter was overconfident.

\textbf{Study 2 (rendered, protocol v0.2).} On \nnTraj{} held out TartanGround desert trajectories with a single scattering sky and white compass noise, an ungated fixed weight compass was worse than no compass on drift at every level because it failed when the sun was nearest the zenith (mean heading RMSE \nhnoonBone{} degrees at 12:30 on 21 June). The weighted schemes cut the heading RMSE at the most severe level from \nthdBzerofour{} to about \nthdBthreefour{} degrees and the drift from \ntdrBzerofour{} to about \ntdrBthreefour{} percent; weighting by the measured DoP beat a gated fixed weight, covariance matching was slightly better overall, and the median position NEES reached \nneBonethree{} against an ideal of 2. The Study~2 results and files are unchanged; its held out set became the Study~3 development set.
