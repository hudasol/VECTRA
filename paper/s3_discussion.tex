\section{Discussion}
\textbf{What survived the stronger tests.} The central Study~2 finding holds on unseen environments, with a sky that is no longer matched to the filter: a weighted sky compass cuts heading error several fold once the cameras degrade, and an ungated one is harmful. Bias makes the ungated compass harmful even with clear images, which Study~1 had already suggested. The choice among weighting schemes matters much less than having one: DoP weighting, covariance matching, the DoP floored covariance matching that Study~2 found by exploration, Huber, two mode and learned weighting all land within a few tenths of a degree, and their order changes between splits. For a designer the robust recommendation is the simplest scheme without a hard gate (B4), because it was selected on one split, confirmed as non inferior on another, and never rejects a sample outright.

\textbf{The blind window is geometry, and its length is uncertain.} The window exists because a polarisation pattern becomes rotationally symmetric when the sun is at the zenith; no sky geometry can remove it, but a ring of off zenith points shrinks it from about an hour to minutes, and only a non celestial cue removes it. Its length depends on the near zenith DoP, where our radiative transfer model and the measured clear sky disagree by about 15 percent: the bracket for Abu Dhabi on 21 June is \sWinAnch{} to \sWinRT{} minutes. A measurement of zenith DoP with the sun within 10 degrees of the zenith, which neither data set we used contains, would close this bracket.

\textbf{Tilt is the practical limit near noon.} When the sun is high, a 2 degree tilt turns into a heading error of several degrees, larger than the photon noise of a sharp sensor. A compass whose noise model ignores tilt is gated out exactly when its geometry should help. Tilt compensation from the IMU attitude \cite{yuan25} and a tilt aware noise model are therefore not refinements but requirements for a ring sensor.

\textbf{The filter is still overconfident, and now we know why.} With an oracle heading the position NEES stays near 50, so the overconfidence comes from the visual translation, whose forward speed is biased by 2 to 4 percent and whose spread is about four times the robust noise level. A scale bias state with a long time constant, or a visual noise level estimated from the spread rather than the median absolute deviation, is the next fix; the short correlation state we tested did not help.

\textbf{Ant style navigation is competitive.} A wheel odometer with a sky compass beat degraded stereo vision without a compass on drift, and in the homing study a tilt aware sky compass kept the error near 2 percent of a 3 km path outside the noon window. This supports the ant inspired architecture as a fallback when cameras fail, with the noon window as its known gap.

\subsection*{Limitations}
Scenes are RENDERED and the TartanGround ground poses are level, so the filter is planar and no SE(3) or external visual inertial baseline was run; the compass, wheel odometry, sun sensor and magnetometer are SIMULATED with assumed parameters; the only MEASURED inputs are one day of Marseille sky images, two published desert DoP values and the aerosol climatology. The aerosol phase matrix is simplified and the dust optical depth is capped at 10. The confirmation split has three environments and 15 trajectories of at most 200 s with three degradation seeds. The weighting schemes differ by less than the variation between scenarios, so their relative order is not established.

\section{Conclusion}
In a frozen confirmation on \sNconf{} unseen rendered trajectories, with a radiative transfer sky, dust tied to the camera degradation, a compass bias and an untuned noise model, a weighted sky compass lowered the heading RMSE of degraded stereo visual odometry from \sHoneB{} to \sHoneA{} degrees, an ungated compass was harmful, a ring of off zenith points improved on the zenith point, and wheel odometry with a sky compass drifted less than vision without one. No sky geometry removes the noon blind window, which we bracket at \sWinAnch{} to \sWinRT{} minutes in Abu Dhabi on 21 June and which occurs at all latitudes within about \sLatMax{} degrees of the equator; a ring shortens it to minutes and only a non celestial cue closes it. The filters remain overconfident because of biased visual translation, not heading. The next steps are a near zenith sky measurement, tilt compensation, a visual scale bias state, and a real rover with a polarisation sensor in the Gulf summer.
