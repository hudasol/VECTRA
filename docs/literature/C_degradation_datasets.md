# C. Degradation, datasets and desert / off-road visual odometry: literature notes

Compiled 2026-11-01. All text was fetched and read in this session (arXiv PDFs via curl then pdftotext; project web pages; DLR open-access PDF; JPL author manuscript). Nothing is cited from memory. Where a DOI appears it was printed on the fetched page or in a fetched reference list, and the place is stated. "Read" levels: FULL = text extracted and the relevant sections read (not every sentence of every section; the sections skipped are named); ABSTRACT = abstract/intro only; WEB = project web page.

## 1. TartanAir (dataset, simulated)

- Citation: Wang W., Zhu D., Wang X., Hu Y., Qiu Y., Wang C., Hu Y., Kapoor A., Scherer S. "TartanAir: A Dataset to Push the Limits of Visual SLAM." arXiv:2003.14338v2 [cs.RO], 8 Aug 2020 (IROS 2020 per common usage, but venue is NOT printed in the extracted text; treat as arXiv). https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.14338
- Read: FULL for Sections I to V (design, data pipeline, baselines, controlled experiment). Appendix tables not read line by line.
- Facts:
  1. Scale: "We have set up 30 environments" and "4TB data, which consists of 1037 long motion sequences. Each ... contains 500-4000 data frames", ">1 million frames".
  2. Modalities in the paper: stereo RGB, depth, segmentation, optical flow, camera poses, simulated LiDAR (built from "4 90°-FOV cameras"). The paper text has NO IMU, no sensor rate, no licence statement (word search for IMU/inertial/licence returned nothing).
  3. Weather/lighting in the paper: "different times-of-day and different seasons, while it is raining, snowing, and foggy." No dust, sand-storm or desert glare is listed in the paper.
  4. Controlled on/off experiment (ORB-SLAM monocular = ORB-M, stereo = ORB-S): "The SR drops more than 50% with the rain and storm for the ORB-M, and 22% with storm for the ORB-S." SR is the success rate (non-lost sequences / all), cut to 200 frames.
  5. Stated limitation on darkness, directly relevant to low-light work: "there are limitations in simulating the dark scene" and synthetic data lack "camera noise or motion blur". Hence dark synthetic frames still give SLAM "many features".
  6. Winter-forest note: snow "results in fewer features on the ground" (low-texture analogue).
- Companion web pages (WEB, opened 2026-11-01):
  - https://tartanair.org/modalities.html : "12 RGB images from 12 cameras", "FoV of 90 degree and a resolution of 640 x 640", "stereo baseline is 0.25 m", "sampled at 10 Hz". Also "tools for adding random noise and motion blur".
  - IMU generation (same page): "IMU ground truth data is generated by interpolating the camera pose" and "double-differentiate the translation pose using a spline for the acceleration"; orientation differentiated with a spline for angular rate; "code to add realistic noise" is provided. So the IMU is derived from the pose spline, not from a physics/sensor model, and is noise-free unless the user adds noise.
  - https://tartanair.org/ (License page text): "TartanAir V2 dataset is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License and the toolkit is licensed under a MIT License." This is the V2 statement from the docs site, not from the 2020 paper.
  - https://tartanair.org/environments.html lists, among others, "Western Desert Town" (Rural, Mix, Large, Day/Night), "Desert GasStation", "Dusty Factory" and "EndofTheWorld". These are the closest to desert content; their realism for sand / Gulf terrain is unverified (images not inspected).

## 2. TartanGround (dataset, simulated, ground robots)

- Citation: Patel M., Yang F., Qiu Y., Cadena C., Scherer S., Hutter M., Wang W. "TartanGround: A Large-Scale Dataset for Ground Robot Perception and Navigation." arXiv:2505.10696v2 [cs.RO], 30 Jul 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10696 ; page cited by authors: https://tartanair.org/tartanground
- Read: FULL for abstract, Section III (dataset), Section IV-B (SLAM evaluation) and conclusion; occupancy-prediction experiments only skimmed.
- Facts:
  1. Counts: "878 trajectories (440 omni-wheeled, 198 diff-wheeled, and 240 legged), each with 600 to 8000 samples", "approximately 15 TB, 1.44 million samples, and 17.3 million RGB images" across 63 environments (Unreal Engine 4 + AirSim, environments from TartanAir-V2). The web page says "~16 TB" (paper and page disagree).
  2. Sensors: "6 stereo RGB cameras (front, left, right, back, top, bottom), each with a fov of 90°"; poses generated "at a fixed frequency of 10 Hz"; page gives 640x640 pixels at 10 Hz and a 32 beam simulated LiDAR.
  3. IMU: only one sentence, "During post-processing, we generate additional ground truth data, including IMU data, optical flow, stereo disparity, LiDAR point clouds". The paper gives NO IMU rate or noise model. For the legged (ANYmal D, Gazebo) runs the paper says it also records "base velocities and accelerations, joint states, and contact forces". The IMU recipe (spline double differentiation, no noise by default) is documented only for TartanAir on the web page above and is presumably shared, but TartanGround's own page was not verified to say so.
  4. Licence: the paper has none. The tartanair.org/tartanground page text contained no licence statement (searched). Only the TartanAir V2 docs state CC BY 4.0 / MIT. Treat TartanGround licence as UNCONFIRMED.
  5. SLAM evaluation (front camera, 4 trajectories, mean relative translation error m/frame): ORB-SLAM3 0.364, DPVO 0.040, MAC-VO 0.012; ORB-SLAM3 rotation error 1.861 deg/frame vs 0.043 for DPVO. ORB-SLAM3 tracking was lost and segments "concatenated". Failure causes named: "occlusion from tall grass and bushes", "indoor-outdoor transitions, bringing dramatic illumination change", "narrow dark stair that lacks good visual features". No desert environment is used.
  6. Limitation stated: "In future, we aim to curate and release task-specific benchmarks" (i.e. no official VO benchmark split yet). Data are synthetic; no sim-to-real study of VO is in the paper.

## 3. ImageNet-C: Hendrycks and Dietterich (image corruption benchmark, definition of severity)

- Citation: Hendrycks D., Dietterich T. "Benchmarking Neural Network Robustness to Common Corruptions and Perturbations." ICLR 2019 (header "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2019"); arXiv:1903.12261v1, 28 Mar 2019. https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.12261
- Read: FULL for Sections 1 to 3 and metrics; appendices A, B skimmed. Parameters below come from the authors' code, opened separately: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hendrycks/robustness/master/ImageNet-C/create_c/make_imagenet_c.py (621 lines).
- Facts:
  1. Structure: "15 types of algorithmically generated corruptions ... Each type of corruption has five levels of severity, resulting in 75 distinct corruptions." Categories: noise, blur, weather (snow, frost, fog, brightness), digital.
  2. The paper gives severity only through example images (Appendix A); numeric definitions live in code. Examples from the code: Gaussian noise sigma = [.08, .12, .18, .26, .38]; contrast factor = [0.4, .3, .2, .1, .05]; fog (amplitude, decay) from (1.5,2) at level 1 to (3,1.4) at level 5; brightness adds [.1, .2, .3, .4, .5] to the HSV V channel, clipped (brightening only, there is no darkening / low-light corruption in the 15).
  3. Metric: Corruption Error is computed per corruption "across five corruption severity levels", normalised by AlexNet, averaged to mCE. Classifiers only; no temporal or pose-estimation tasks.
  4. Gaps for this project: no dust haze, no sand-colour low-contrast, no sun glare or lens flare, no lens soiling. Frost is described as "ice crystals" on a lens. Corruptions are applied per still image with random variation, so they are not temporally coherent for VO.

## 4. "Failure or Drift?" (SLAM corruption benchmark)

- Citation: Thomas A. S., Agnihotri S., Keuper M. "Failure or Drift? Evaluating Monocular SLAM under Synthetic and Real-World Corruptions." arXiv:2608.30690v1 [cs.CV], 31 Aug 2026 (preprint; no venue printed). https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30690
- Read: FULL for abstract, introduction, Section 3 (datasets, corruptions, protocol), Sections 4.1 to 4.3 and Limitations. Section 5 (transfer to real data) read in part.
- Facts:
  1. Design: ORB-SLAM2, DPVO, DROID-SLAM on KITTI sequence 00 under 13 image-space corruptions (imagecorruptions package, i.e. ImageNet-C style), 7 geometry-aware (3D Common Corruptions: far/near focus, flash, fog, ISO noise, low light, rain), 3 compound; "Each family has five nominal severity levels" (65 + 35 + 15 variants). Real counterpart: four 4Seasons sequences (clear, rain, winter/fog-like, evening).
  2. Failure vs drift: ORB-SLAM2 "valid in 37 of 63 image-space attempts, 15 of 23 geometry-aware attempts, and 10 of 15 compound attempts"; Gaussian noise, impulse noise and snow gave no evaluable ORB-SLAM2 trajectory at any severity. DPVO and DROID-SLAM stayed valid in every attempt, "while their aligned APE frequently exceeds 100 m".
  3. Proxy fidelity matters: DPVO has lower APE in 30 of 43 paired image-space settings, but DROID-SLAM is lower in all 33 geometry-aware settings. Quote: "severity orders the corruption parameters, not necessarily the estimation difficulty." Under geometry-aware rain DROID-SLAM APE falls from 111.9 m (severity 1) to 87.9 m (severity 5).
  4. Own limitations: "one KITTI trajectory and one archived execution per condition"; monocular only; "does not cover ... inertial fusion". Not desert, not off-road, no IMU.
- Related works named in its introduction (not opened): Measuring Robustness of Visual SLAM, Adversarial Lab, 3D Common Corruptions, RobustSpring (see UNOPENED).

## 5. Measuring robustness of Visual SLAM (Prokhorov et al.)

- Citation: Prokhorov D., Zhukov D., Vorontsova A., Barinova O., Konushin A. "Measuring robustness of Visual SLAM." arXiv:1910.04755v1, 10 Oct 2019 (Samsung AI Center Moscow). https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04755
- Read: FULL (short paper).
- Facts: it is an indoor RGB-D ORB-SLAM2 study on TUM RGB-D, ICL-NUIM, InteriorNet and a new "HomeRobot" synthetic set (25 Hz, 1,000 images per trajectory); it varies agent speed ("lower accuracy ... due to reduction of the overlap between the consecutive frames"). It is NOT an image-corruption benchmark. Low relevance; cited only so the team does not mistake it for one.

## 6. Mars Exploration Rover visual odometry (Maimone, Cheng, Matthies)

- Citation: Maimone M., Cheng Y., Matthies L. "Two Years of Visual Odometry on the Mars Exploration Rovers." Journal of Field Robotics 24(3): 169-186, 2007, doi:10.1002/rob.20184. The volume, pages and DOI come from the reference list on https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_odometry (fetched); the PDF itself is an author manuscript (rob-06-0081.R4) at https://robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/media/documents/rob-06-0081.R4.pdf and prints no venue. Verify the DOI against the publisher before final citation.
- Read: FULL (24 pages text extracted; Sections 1 to 6 read where relevant).
- Facts:
  1. Algorithm and performance: stereo "256x256" NAVCAM pairs, "high rates of successful convergence (97% on Spirit, 95% on Opportunity)", "slip ratios as high as 125%". Opportunity converged "95% (828/875) of the time" in its first period.
  2. Sand and low texture: "The feature detector was optimized for feature-laden natural terrain, and often failed to find sufficient unique features in the piles of sand". Convergence succeeded only because wheel tracks gave features ("tracks left by the vehicle ... provided enough features").
  3. Meridiani Planum: "difficult or impossible to find a patch of nearby terrain that has enough texture ... thick layer of extremely fine particles". Authors note such smooth areas are flat, where IMU+wheel odometry was adequate.
  4. Non-convergence causes: "too large a motion (e.g. commanding a 40 degree turn in place ...) or ... a lack of features". Heading source: the onboard IMU "drift rate (usually less than 3 degrees per hour)", so VO was "typically used to update rover position only". VO was used rarely (run on short drives, slopes, wheel drag, sand piles) because of 20 MHz CPU cost of about three minutes per step.
  5. Shadow note: the paper has a figure of the rover's own shadow with tracked features (Fig. 13). I did not read a quantitative effect; do not claim one.

## 7. MADMAX: Moroccan desert Mars-analogue dataset (Meyer et al.)

- Citation: Meyer L., Smisek M., Fontan Villacampa A., Oliva Maza L., Medina D., Schuster M., Steidle F., Vayugundla M., Mueller M. G., Rebele B., Wedler A., Triebel R. "The MADMAX data set for visual-inertial rover navigation on Mars." Journal of Field Robotics, 2021, doi:10.1002/rob.22016 (DOI printed on the DLR record https://elib.dlr.de/141662 ). Open-access PDF read: https://elib.dlr.de/141662/1/rob.22016.pdf . Data: https://rmc.dlr.de/morocco2018 (as printed in the abstract; the data site itself was NOT opened).
- Read: FULL for abstract, Sections 3 to 6 (sensor unit, ground truth, disturbances, ORB-SLAM2 / VINS-Mono results); related-work sections skimmed.
- Facts:
  1. Content: "36 different navigation experiments, captured at eight Mars analog sites"; "longest trajectory covers 1.5 km and the combined trajectory length is 9.2 km"; sites near Erfoud, northern Sahara, from "sandy and featureless through pebbly to rocky" including "sandy dune fields". Licence: "open access article under the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution License" (that is the article licence; the dataset licence was not seen).
  2. Sensors: monochrome navigation stereo "14 Hz ... 1032 x 772 px ... auto exposure"; colour camera 4 Hz, 2064x1544; omni fisheye 4-8 Hz; IMU "XSENS MTi-10, 100 Hz". Ground truth: GNSS "1 Hz" RTK from two body antennas, fused to a "6 DoF reference, sampled at 100 Hz", i.e. a dual-antenna heading ground truth. No magnetometer or sun sensor in the sensor table; each run starts with the platform "oriented to the east using a spirit level and a compass" for rough alignment only.
  3. Optical disturbances: "lens flares due to a low sun position" in afternoon runs, "strong over or underexposure of image regions", and the sensor unit's own moving shadow which "introduces the similar optical features" that ORB-SLAM2/VINS-Mono track. Frame drops 5 to 10 percent (15 to 19 percent for F runs).
  4. Baselines: ORB-SLAM2 (stereo) and VINS-Mono. Findings: with inter-frame overlap below "~80%" plus challenging visual conditions, ORB-SLAM2 loses tracking; "VINS-Mono is more robust against tracking failures produced by visual effects" because the IMU "is able to bridge the gaps". Run A6 failed for both despite 5 percent frame drops. Metrics only used when "at least 75% of the trajectory" completed.
  5. Companion: Wudenka et al., arXiv:2109.05509, "Towards Robust Monocular Visual Odometry for Flying Robots on Planetary Missions" (FULL abstract/intro/Section VI): "highly repetitive textures present in the wastelands of Mars pose a huge challenge to descriptor matching based approaches"; on MADMAX "ORB-SLAM3 fails at random points in time" while their KLT-based method "only looses track when frame drops and fast motion coincide". Venue not printed in the text I read (an IEEE copyright block is present).

## 8. Off-road driving datasets

### 8a. TartanDrive 2.0
- Citation: Sivaprakasam M., Maheshwari P., Guaman Castro M., Triest S., Nye M., Willits S., Saba A., Wang W., Scherer S. "TartanDrive 2.0: More Modalities and Better Infrastructure to Further Self-Supervised Learning Research in Off-Road Driving Tasks." arXiv:2402.01913v1, 2 Feb 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01913
- Read: FULL for abstract, Sections II to IV (sensors, rates).
- Facts: "seven hours of data at speeds of up to 15m/s"; "255 acres"; MultiSense S21 stereo, greyscale plus RGB "at 10Hz each"; "NovAtel PROPAK-V3-RT2i GNSS provides IMU data at 100Hz", fused pose "at 50Hz", MultiSense IMU "400Hz"; LiDAR 10 Hz. ### 8b. RELLIS-3D
- Citation: Jiang P., Osteen P., Wigness M., Saripalli S. "RELLIS-3D Dataset: Data, Benchmarks and Analysis." arXiv:2011.12954v4, 25 May 2022. https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12954
- Read: ABSTRACT + sensor/dataset sections.
- Facts: Texas A&M Rellis Campus; "13,556 LiDAR scans and 6,235 images" annotated (semantic labels); RGB Basler acA1920-50gc "1920x1200, 10 hz"; "Vectornav VN300 Dual Antenna GNSS/INS, 300 Hz GPS, 100 Hz IMU"; "five sequences". It is a segmentation dataset first; nothing on VO failures; no magnetometer reported; no licence in paper; vegetated, not desert.

### 8c. Boreas
- Citation: Burnett K., Yoon D. J., Wu Y., Li A. Z., Zhang H., Lu S., Qian J., Tseng W.-K., Lambert A., Leung K. Y. K., Schoellig A. P., Barfoot T. D. "Boreas: A Multi-Season Autonomous Driving Dataset." arXiv:2203.10168v2, 26 Jan 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10168
- Read: abstract, dataset description, sensor spec list.
- Facts: "over 350km of driving data"; "5MP FLIR Blackfly S camera" at 2448x2048, 10 Hz; Applanix POS LV IMU "200Hz"; rain, "falling snow", and one snow-on-ground sequence; centimetre ground truth by post-processed GNSS/IMU (RTX). Useful for weather, not for desert; the camera is not tied to a heading reference beyond the INS.

## 9. Planetary / lunar / degraded-visual-environment evidence

### 9a. Lunar front-end matching
- Citation: Casadesus Vila G., Gao G. "Evaluation of Front-End Feature Matching for Lunar Visual SLAM." arXiv:2610.30774v1, 22 Oct 2026 (preprint). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30774
- Read: ABSTRACT + introduction.
- Facts: "eleven classical and learned detector-matcher combinations on nine synthetic, lunar-testbed, and terrestrial-analogue datasets"; "regolith is low in texture"; classical detectors "depend on lighting while SuperPoint changes little: a low spotlight makes them more accurate than diffuse light"; "synthetic results alone do not establish relative performance on real rover imagery". Useful support for the warning that simulated VO results may not transfer.

### 9b. Dust as an obscurant for visible-light VIO
- Citation: Khattak S., Papachristos C., Alexis K. "Visual-Thermal Landmarks and Inertial Fusion for Navigation in Degraded Visual Environments." arXiv:1903.01656v1, 5 Mar 2019. https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01656
- Read: introduction + method motivation (partial), experiments not read.
- Facts: "poor illumination, low texture, or presence of obscurants including fog, smoke and dust, the reliability of visible light cameras deteriorates significantly"; "airborne dust can also temporarily introduce strong local gradients which can lead to the selection of non-repeatable features"; "may lead to tracking failure". Tested in a dark mine "in the presence of heavy airborne dust" with an aerial robot. This is a mechanism statement; I did not extract tracking-failure percentages.

### 9c. Others opened but not used
- 2603.18589 (Choi et al., LiDAR-inertial-visual odometry features under low-light, overexposure): ABSTRACT + intro only; states photometric methods "fail to converge" under illumination change; no desert data. Not recommended as a key citation.
- 2610.20958 (Line-augmented cuVSLAM): abstract only; warehouse low-texture, not outdoor.
- 2502.09795 (Pisanti et al., Mars helicopter map-based localisation under illumination): abstract only; aerial and map-based.

## 10. Heading references with images (question 4)

- Result: no ground-robot dataset I could open provides camera images together with a magnetometer, sun-compass or polarisation heading reference as dedicated channels. Specifically:
  - MADMAX: dual-antenna GNSS gives heading ground truth, IMU 100 Hz, no magnetometer (Section 7).
  - TartanDrive 2.0, RELLIS-3D, Boreas: GNSS/INS orientation only, no magnetometer listed in the papers.
  - TartanAir / TartanGround: simulated; IMU derived from pose; no magnetometer, sun or polarisation channel documented.
- Polarisation / sky heading evidence (opened):
  - Potter B., Mounier E., Noureldin A., Antar Y., Alam M. "Navigation by Skylight Polarization via Rayleigh Model Optimization on a Road Vehicle." arXiv:2610.20797v1, 14 Oct 2026 (preprint). READ: abstract, method, experiment set-up. Facts: LUCID PHX050S-P/Q polarisation camera pointing "to the zenith", "2048 by 2448 image every 200 ms", 120 deg field of view; reference "NovAtel PwrPak7 ... dual GNSS antennas" at 50 Hz; three urban trajectories in Kingston, Canada (10.10 min, 18.92 min, and a third), 24 Nov 2025, "sunny with few passing clouds"; "sub-degree RMS yaw error ... 0.718 to 0.889 deg". The paper text I searched has no statement that the data are released. Not desert; clear-sky only; wheeled car, not a small ground robot.
  - Liang H., Bai H. "Polarized Skylight Navigation Simulation (PSNS) Dataset." arXiv:2007.13081v5, 2020. READ: abstract and structure. Facts: it is a simulated dataset (sun-position, Berry and Hosek sky models; "LI, DOP and AOP images"), with code on GitHub (two repositories named in the paper). No real images. It supports testing sky-compass algorithms but not real-image heading.
- Also seen in search result lists but not opened: 2107.02328, 2010.10104, 2012.09154, 2007.08987 (polarised skylight orientation; see UNOPENED).

## 11. Camera soiling / dust (question 5)

- Uricar M., Sistu G., Rashed H., Vobecky A., Ravi Kumar V., Krizek P., Burger F., Yogamani S. "Let's Get Dirty: GAN Based Data Augmentation for Camera Lens Soiling Detection in Autonomous Driving." arXiv:1912.02249v3, 14 Nov 2020. READ: abstract, Sections 1 to 4.3 (relevant parts). Facts: cameras "can get soiled very easily by mud, dust, water, frost"; on synthetic soiling a DeepLabV3+ model trained on clean images "records 56.6% mIoU on clean test data and 34.8% on soiled data, a performance drop of 21.8%". The degradation is measured on semantic segmentation with generated (synthetic) soiling, not on VO. Good as a perception-level primary source; it is not evidence about odometry error.
- Uricar M., Krizek P., Sistu G., Yogamani S. "SoilingNet: Soiling Detection on Automotive Surround-View Cameras." arXiv:1905.01492v2, 17 Jul 2019. READ: abstract and dataset section. Facts: "Cameras have a much higher degradation in performance due to soiling compared to other sensors"; "76,448" images collected; classification accuracy only. Gives no VO numbers.
- Result: I found NO primary source quantifying the effect of dust/soiling on a camera for visual odometry or VIO. The only VO-adjacent dust statement is the airborne-dust mechanism in 9b.

## Synthesis (10 lines)

1. Established: feature-based VO loses tracking in low-texture sand, fine dust, and low-contrast scenes; MER VO often "failed to find sufficient unique features in the piles of sand" and relied on wheel tracks or flat-terrain IMU/odometry.
2. Established: in a real Sahara analogue (MADMAX) low sun flare, over/under exposure, own shadow and frame drops with overlap below about 80% break ORB-SLAM2; VINS-Mono bridges gaps via the 100 Hz IMU.
3. Established: learned trackers (DPVO, DROID-SLAM) tend to keep outputting poses under corruption and drift (APE above 100 m on KITTI-00) instead of failing, so failure detection must be separate.
4. Established: severity is a parameter ordering, not a difficulty ordering; synthetic proxy fidelity can reverse method rankings (Failure or Drift?), and simple ImageNet-C style brightness/contrast tests mislead.
5. Gap: ImageNet-C has no darkening, dust haze, sun glare, or soiling corruption and is not temporally coherent; the physically-motivated 3D-CC rain/fog/low-light are closest but are road/KITTI only.
6. Gap: no primary source found that quantifies camera dust or lens soiling on VO/VIO error; soiling evidence is segmentation on synthetic dirt (21.8 point mIoU drop), and airborne dust evidence is mechanistic (Khattak et al.).
7. Gap: TartanAir itself says dark scenes are poorly simulated (no sensor noise or blur) and weather is rain, snow, fog only; there is no sand storm, dust or glare model, so results there will be optimistic for desert.
8. Usable for a later real stage: MADMAX (real Sahara, stereo+IMU, dual-GNSS 6DoF ground truth, 9.2 km; CC BY article, dataset licence to check) is the best desert-like option; TartanDrive 2.0 and RELLIS-3D give off-road IMU/GNSS but vegetated terrain; Boreas gives weather but urban.
9. Usable for simulation stage: TartanAir V2 (CC BY 4.0 per docs; IMU is spline-derived and noise free unless added; has "Western Desert Town" and dusty scenes) and TartanGround (ground-robot trajectories, IMU mentioned but unspecified, licence unconfirmed).
10. Heading references: no opened ground-robot dataset provides images with magnetometer, sun-compass or polarisation channels; the nearest are MADMAX (dual-antenna GNSS heading) and a 2025 urban polarisation-camera trajectory set (arXiv:2610.20797, release not stated), so a sun/polarisation heading cue would need its own data collection or simulated sky models (PSNS).
